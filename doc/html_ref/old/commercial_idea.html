Teardown: A simple pulse oximeter for home healthcare

Teardown ( MAY 26, 2020 BY LEE TESCHLER)

Summary: The paper disassembles a low-cost fingertip pulse oximeter, analyzes its circuit design and components, and explains how its LEDs, photodiode, and microcontroller work together to measure oxygen saturation and pulse rate efficiently in a compact, low-power device. It runs via an MCU that does the calculations and generates the PWM signal to drive the LEDs. The heart of the device is a Silicon Labs C8051F007 mixed-signal SoC MCU with a 12-bit multi-channel ADC. Among its features are a programmable-gain pre-amp, two 12-bit DACs, two voltage comparators, a voltage reference, and an 8051-compatible MCU core with 32 kbytes of Flash memory. There are also four 16-bit timers and four byte-wide general-purpose digital port I/O, 2,304 bytes of RAM and executes at up to 25 MIPS.

Cheap Fingertip Pulse Oximeter Teardown

Kean Electronics

Summary: “LK87” is the trade name for a popular fingertip SpO₂ meter, manufactured by many manufacturers. So the MCU inside is not fixed—it depends on the lot/manufacturer. In fact, teardowns show that different MCUs have been used in oximeters of this model. For this specific teardowns we can clearly see that the MCU is TI MSP430F415.

Appearance

Teardowns show that different units use different chips—for example, some use the TI MSP430F415 (16-bit RISC with LCD driver), others the Silicon Labs C8051F007 (8051 core with 12-bit ADC/DAC), and many newer versions adopt Holtek MCUs like the BH66F2560 or HT32F59045, which integrate pulse-oximeter analog front-ends. Thus, there is no single “LK87 MCU”; the hardware platform differs across production runs.

C8051F007 (from Silicon Labs)

C8051F0xx Datasheet

Summary: The C8051F007 is an 8-bit 8051-compatible mixed-signal MCU with excellent analog front-end (12-bit ADC/DAC, temperature sensor) and ~25 MIPS throughput, but with limited arithmetic hardware (8-bit ALU, basic multiply/divide, no FPU or DSP extensions), so it’s optimum for sensor/analog tasks rather than heavy computational loads.
